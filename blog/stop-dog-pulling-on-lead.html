How to Stop Your Dog Pulling on the Lead

Your dog pulls because pulling works. Everything below is about making it stop working.

A dog that drags you down Mulcture Hall Road is not being disobedient. They have simply learned the most reliable rule of their day: pull, and the world arrives faster.

Every walk where pulling gets them to the lamp post has been a training session. You are not starting from zero; you are competing with months of successful practice.

The one rule that does the work

A tight lead means we stop. A loose lead means we go.

That is the entire method. The hard part is being boringly consistent, because it only works if it is true every single time. One walk where pulling still works resets a lot of progress.

How to actually do it

  1. Start in the house and garden, where there is nothing interesting. Yes, really. You cannot teach this for the first time on a pavement full of smells.
  2. The moment the lead tightens, stop dead. Do not yank, do not talk. Become a lamp post.
  3. Wait. Your dog will eventually ease off or glance back. The instant the lead softens, say "yes" and walk on. Walking on IS the reward.
  4. Pay the position you want. Every few steps your dog spends beside you, feed a treat at your leg. You are building a habit, not bribing.
  5. Add difficulty slowly. Garden, then quiet street, then busier street. Skip a stage and it falls apart.

Expect it to get worse first

For the first week your walks will be short, stop-start and slightly humiliating. You will cover about 100 metres. This is normal and it is temporary — you are removing a rule your dog has relied on for a long time, and they will try harder before they try something else.

Plan for it: do the training on short deliberate sessions, and take a separate "sniffing walk" on a long line somewhere open so your dog still gets a proper outing.

Kit that helps — and kit that does not

Use: a well-fitted Y-shaped harness with a front clip, and a fixed two-metre lead. A double-ended lead clipped front and back gives you real steering with no pain.

Avoid: retractable leads (they teach that tension is normal and gets you further — the exact opposite of the rule), and anything that works by hurting — prong collars, choke chains, shock collars. They suppress pulling by making walks unpleasant, and the fallout usually shows up later as a dog who is anxious about going out.

If your dog only pulls towards other dogs

That is not a lead-walking problem, it is frustration or fear, and the method above will not fix it on its own. Have a read of our piece on lunging on the lead instead.

How long it takes

Two to three weeks of short, consistent sessions for most dogs. Longer for strong adolescents who have been pulling for a year. The dogs that never crack it are almost always the ones whose owners were consistent on weekdays and gave up at weekends.
