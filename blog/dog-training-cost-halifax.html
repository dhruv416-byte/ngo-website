How Much Does Dog Training Cost in Halifax?

Real numbers, no "contact us for pricing". Here is what training actually costs around Calderdale — and how to pay less.

Almost every dog training website in West Yorkshire hides its prices behind a contact form. We think that is unhelpful, so here are real figures.

These are typical 2026 rates across Halifax, Brighouse, Elland and Sowerby Bridge — not just ours. Use them to sanity-check any quote you are given.

Group puppy or obedience classes

Around £12–£20 per session, usually sold as a course of four to six weeks, so £60–£110 all in.

Good value if your dog copes around other dogs. Poor value if your dog spends the whole hour barking — a stressed dog learns nothing, and you have paid for six weeks of it.

One-to-one training

£45–£75 per hour. Most families need three to five sessions for a specific problem, so budget £150–£350.

This is the right choice for anything embarrassing or specific: pulling, recall, jumping at visitors, or a dog that cannot cope in class.

Behaviour consultations

£90–£180 for a first assessment, usually 90 minutes to two hours, often at your home. Follow-ups are typically £50–£80.

Reactivity, aggression, separation anxiety and resource guarding sit here. It costs more because it takes longer, involves a written plan, and often means liaising with your vet.

What we charge

Many of our places are free or subsidised, funded by donations. Where we do charge, fees sit at or below the bottom of every range above, and every penny goes back into training places for families who could not otherwise afford one.

We will always tell you the cost — if any — before you commit to anything. There is no sales call.

Three ways to spend less

  • Ask about subsidised places first. Most people never ask. Funding exists precisely so cost is not the reason a dog gets rehomed.
  • Do not buy a package you cannot finish. Two sessions you practise between beat six you attend passively.
  • Start earlier than feels necessary. A £60 puppy course is dramatically cheaper than a £300 behaviour plan for the same dog at two years old.

What should worry you about a quote

  • Guaranteed results. Nobody can guarantee a living animal's behaviour. Anyone who does is selling.
  • Big upfront packages with no assessment. A trainer should know your dog before selling you ten sessions.
  • Equipment you must buy from them, especially anything described as a "training collar" that delivers a shock, spray or prong.
  • No qualifications and no insurance. Dog training is unregulated in the UK — anyone can call themselves a trainer. Ask.

Cheap training that frightens your dog is the most expensive option available, because you end up paying someone else to undo it.
